# Build a Portfolio That Reads Like a CV

The best 2025-2026 pattern is a calm, typographic, single-column page. Real HTML text carries the CV content. Two or three case studies carry the proof. A print stylesheet and a text-based PDF turn the same page into an ATS-safe, one-to-two-page CV. For engineers, one page is the default length, and two pages are normal at roughly 6-8+ years of experience as long as page two holds real substance ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474607/engineering-resume-one-page-vs-two/); [Interview Kickstart](https://www.interviewkickstart.com/blog/how-to-structure-your-software-engineering-resume)). Recruiters decide in about **7.4 seconds** whether to keep reading ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/)). The top third of the page must therefore show title, employer, stack and one impact line. On the web, the same logic puts identity, positioning, best work and contact links in the first screen ([GreatFrontEnd](https://www.greatfrontend.com/blog/frontend-developer-portfolio)). Visual trends favour one committed idea, strong type and restrained, accessible motion, and they treat particle-field heroes as clichés ([svilenkovic.com](https://www.svilenkovic.com/3d/3d-portfolio-trends-2026)). Much of the evidence is career-blog material rather than vendor documentation or recruiter surveys. Where the notes lack a source, this report labels the advice as engineering judgement.

## A one-to-two-page, single-column CV with standard headings survives both humans and parsers

The sourced guidance on CV structure is consistent but thin on one point. Sources agree on length and on layout. They do not settle whether summary, skills or experience should come first. The one relevant caution is that skills and projects placed below experience on page two can be missed, so Interview Kickstart suggests a hybrid layout with a compact skills block on page one. It also says two pages are the norm for experienced and senior engineers ([Interview Kickstart](https://www.interviewkickstart.com/blog/how-to-structure-your-software-engineering-resume)). A second page is justified by multiple related roles with measurable impact, open source, patents or technical leadership ([Namastedev](https://namastedev.com/topics/bonus-resume-masterclass/one-page-vs-two-page-resume-developer)). One source claims FAANG and high-growth startups prefer one page below senior staff level, but that rests on a single source ([loopcv](https://www.loopcv.pro/es/guides/one-page-vs-two-page-resume)). A practical order for a tech lead with 6+ years is: header, a two-line summary, compact grouped skills, reverse-chronological experience, selected projects or side business, then education and certifications. That order is this report's synthesis, not a cited standard.

Layout matters because of how recruiters read. The Ladders eye-tracking study reported a **7.4-second** average initial screen, up from about 6 seconds in 2012. Simple layouts with clear headings, bold titles and bullets held attention, while clutter, little white space, multiple columns and long sentences hurt ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/)). The study is from 2018, and critics note that 7.4 seconds measures the decision to keep reading, not the full evaluation ([The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=17493)). The practical point is that the top third of page one has to work as a standalone pitch.

ATS advice is more nuanced than folklore suggests. The reliable rules are standard headings (Work Experience, Education, Skills, Certifications, Professional Summary), a single column, no tables, text boxes, sidebars, icons, photos or skill-rating bars, contact details in the body rather than in headers or footers, and common fonts at 10-12pt ([The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=6248); [Scale.jobs](https://scale.jobs/blog/creating-ats-friendly-resum); [SCU Career Center](https://www.scu.edu/careercenter/toolkit/job-scan-common-ats-resume-formatting-mistakes/)). The claim that a robot silently rejects 75% of resumes is described as largely folklore, with hard cutoffs rare outside high-volume retail and call-center hiring ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233476076/check-if-ats-can-read-your-resume/)). The real risks are parse failure and keyword-based recruiter search. Workday penalises parse failures more than Greenhouse and relies on keyword search, so mirroring the job ad's wording pays off ([atsverification.com](https://atsverification.com/blog/greenhouse-vs-workday-ats-parsing/)). Sources conflict on whether Greenhouse can auto-reject: one says only through employer-configured rules ([Enhancv](https://enhancv.com/blog/how-does-greenhouse-work/)), another says never ([Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/work/ats-insider)). They also conflict on file format, with some favouring text-based PDF and others DOCX ([Scale.jobs](https://scale.jobs/blog/ats-resume-format-works-2026) versus The Interview Guys). The safe rule is to follow each application's instructions and never submit a scanned image.

For leadership roles, the guidance is to open each role with a scope line covering team size, budget and platform area. Bullets then follow an Action + Context + Quantified Result pattern with verbs such as built, scaled, led and enabled ([Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/de-de/academy/engineering-manager-resume)). That source targets engineering managers, not tech leads, so treat it as an adapted template. A usable bullet form is "Led/Built X for a team of N, using Y, resulting in metric Z." Group skills into categories such as Languages, Frameworks, Cloud and DevOps, Data, and Leadership. For a side business, use a concrete title and concrete results. Short-tenure ventures can read as lack of persistence, so frame outcomes ([LiveCareer](https://www.livecareer.com/resume-examples/co-founder)). Keep it to two or three bullets and name any software you built with its stack (the framing as proof of ownership is this report's inference).

Regional conventions can conflict with ATS rules. Bangkok CVs commonly carry a photo, and DOB or nationality sometimes appears ([VisualCV Thailand](https://visualcv.com/international/thailand); [Ajarn](https://www.ajarn.com/blogs/stephen-louw/a-suitable-resume)). ATS guidance says to avoid photos. The resolution is two versions, a no-photo ATS PDF for online portals and an optional photo version for direct or recruiter submission. That split is an inference from the two sourced positions.

## The portfolio page works when it leads with a lane and proof, then shows experience as crawlable text

The portfolio evidence points to a short page with a clear target role, a few deep projects, and visible contact paths. GreatFrontEnd's recommended order is Home (lane, seniority, strongest work, contact links), Selected work (2-4 case studies), Technical notes, Code samples, Experience, then Contact. It says reviewers spend "only a few minutes" and look for a clear target level and role, personal contribution separated from the team's, runnable demos, and claims that match evidence ([GreatFrontEnd](https://www.greatfrontend.com/blog/frontend-developer-portfolio)). Scrimba adds 3-5 polished projects, about five pages in total, 90+ Lighthouse scores, and no heavy animation or skill bars. It is a course vendor, so weigh it accordingly ([Scrimba](https://scrimba.com/articles/how-to-build-a-web-developer-portfolio-that-gets-you-hired)). A Profy.dev survey of 60+ hiring managers, reported secondhand, found **93%** would look at a portfolio if provided, but **51%** said a candidate's chances would not drop without one. That makes the site a container for content, not a substitute for it (same Scrimba source).

For a single static page that doubles as a CV, the notes' synthesis is this sequence: hero (name, role, one-line positioning, location or availability, primary call to action), two to four featured projects, experience timeline, grouped skills without bars, optional writing or open source, a short about, and contact in the footer. Put a CV download link in the header and hero. Putting experience in HTML text keeps it crawlable and printable, with the PDF as a convenience. This sequence is inference from the articles, because no audit of top-ranked portfolio sites (Awwwards, Godly, Siteinspire) was done.

Projects work best as a hybrid. GreatFrontEnd says to use case studies, not project cards, covering problem, role, decisions, tradeoffs, quality checks, result and next steps, with specifics such as "moved filters into the URL so teammates could share table views" instead of "built a dashboard with React." It advises anonymising private work, rebuilding a simplified UI with fake data, and never inventing metrics ([GreatFrontEnd](https://www.greatfrontend.com/blog/frontend-developer-portfolio)). Scrimba's template is problem, approach, challenges, result, and what you would change, with a live link and repo for every project. Reviewers trust case studies that admit constraints and bugs more than polished success stories. Compact cards on the home page should link to short case studies for the top two or three projects. The notes found no evidence on whether separate pages beat in-page expansion.

The turn-offs are consistent across sources: tutorial clones, dead demo links, screenshots without reasoning, vague roles ("worked on the product"), stale content, animations that delay content, and hard-to-find contact links ([Scrimba](https://scrimba.com/articles/how-to-build-a-web-developer-portfolio-that-gets-you-hired); [NTC](https://ntc.it.com/blog/hiring-managers-portfolio); [GreatFrontEnd](https://www.greatfrontend.com/blog/frontend-developer-portfolio)). Most of these sources are secondhand blog summaries, with few first-person hiring-manager quotes. Trust signals to build in are working live and repo links, dated entries, real employer names where allowed, attributed quotes, a visible last-updated date, and plain-text email plus GitHub and LinkedIn.

## Typography-led design with one accent and gated motion is the 2026 direction

The trend sources are weak, mostly blog roundups, so the direction is more reliable than any specific recipe. 2026 portfolios are described as moving from static galleries to interactive, proof-of-work pages that show process, since AI makes polished UI cheap ([Muzli](https://muz.li/blog/beyond-the-pixel-why-your-2026-portfolio-needs-a-dna-and-a-community/); [UX Tools](https://www.uxtools.co/blog/the-portfolio-is-becoming-a-playground)). One 3D-portfolio analysis recommends committing to one visual idea, giving each project a scroll-driven chapter instead of a thumbnail, treating mobile as a priority, and flags the "particle field hero with rotating logo" as overused. It also calls premium typography a craft signal and says strong sites respect `prefers-reduced-motion` ([svilenkovic.com](https://www.svilenkovic.com/3d/3d-portfolio-trends-2026)). Bento-grid cards, large editorial type, a monospace accent for metadata, a restrained palette with one accent, and sparing grain or gradients are this report's inference from that direction and common practice, not sourced trends. Skill bars, autoplay video, heavy parallax and everywhere-glassmorphism are the risky choices; the first three are partly sourced, the last is judgement. Since the page must also print, a calm typographic base with decoration reserved for screen is the sound choice.

Font pairing evidence is generic. Sources say to pair faces that are harmonious but different, with body text prioritising endurance and headings carrying personality ([Digital Synopsis](https://digitalsynopsis.com/design/best-google-fonts-combinations-typeface-pairings/)). DreamHost lists Lato with Merriweather, Playfair Display with Lato, and Nunito with Space Mono ([DreamHost](https://www.dreamhost.com/blog/best-google-fonts/)). The following suggestions are unsourced and should be verified on Google Fonts: Inter or Inter Tight body with Space Grotesk headings and JetBrains Mono metadata, or Fraunces headings over Inter. Use at most two families, prefer variable fonts, and use `clamp()` for fluid sizes.

Platform support decides which effects are safe. `oklch()` and `color-mix()` are Baseline Widely available since May 2023, and `:has()` since December 2023 ([MDN oklch](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/oklch); [MDN color-mix](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/color-mix); [MDN :has](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/:has)). Container queries are usable with a media-query fallback ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/CSS_containment/Container_queries)). Scroll-driven animations are not Baseline. They shipped in Chrome and Edge 115, and Safari 26 is listed in Chrome's badges ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/scroll-driven-animations)). Firefox status is unresolved, with sources conflicting. Use them only as enhancement inside `@supports (animation-timeline: scroll())`, with content visible by default. Cross-document `@view-transition` works in Chrome and Edge 126 and Safari 18.2, not Firefox, and adds little to a one-page site ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@view-transition)). Same-document `startViewTransition` is Baseline 2025 and suits a theme toggle ([MDN ViewTransition](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/ViewTransition)).

Motion and contrast rules are well sourced. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, remove non-essential movement such as parallax, zoom, animated gradients and autoplay. Put animation inside a `no-preference` query, and shorten durations to about 1ms in a global override instead of `animation: none` so `animationend` handlers still fire. JS and Web Animations API animations need their own `matchMedia` check ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion)). Text needs **4.5:1** contrast, or **3:1** for large text, and ratios must not be rounded ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). Accent colours often fail on dark backgrounds, so check every pair in both themes. Animate `transform` and `opacity` only ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/scroll-driven-animations)).

## Performance, SEO and print support make the page a real CV

Core Web Vitals "good" thresholds at the 75th percentile are LCP at or under **2.5 s**, INP at or under **200 ms** and CLS at or under **0.1** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). For LCP, keep the hero resource discoverable in HTML, use `fetchpriority="high"` on only the likely LCP image, never lazy-load it, preload fonts used by a text LCP element, and avoid `font-display: auto` or `block` ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp)). A framework-free static site meets these easily with one self-hosted WOFF2 or a system stack, AVIF or WebP images with explicit width and height, and `defer`red scripts (checklist items are inference).

For discoverability, Google documents `ProfilePage` structured data with a required `mainEntity` Person and recommended `sameAs`, `image` and `dateModified` ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page)). No data shows it helps a personal portfolio rank. Complete Open Graph and Twitter tags help sharing. The 1200x630 OG size and `summary_large_image` come from secondary sources, so check them against primary documentation ([insertchat](https://insertchat.com/free-tools/twitter-card-generator)). Add semantic landmarks, one `h1`, a skip link, `:focus-visible` styles, descriptive link text, a `lang` attribute and dark mode via `prefers-color-scheme`. These baselines are inference.

The print layout is what lets one page serve as the CV. `@page` controls size and margins, and `break-inside` and `break-before` control where entries split. Check print preview, and avoid absolutely positioned layout, where break properties do not work ([CodeShack](https://codeshack.io/references/css/page/); [HTMLCSSToImage](https://htmlcsstoimage.com/blog/optimize-html-for-pdf-printing)). Tutorials target Chromium, and other engines differ. The notes' suggested skeleton is untested. It forces light colours, hides navigation, cursor, canvas and video, strips animation, blur and shadows, keeps jobs and projects unbroken, expands link URLs, and flattens bento grids. Pair it with a text-based PDF linked as "Download CV (PDF, 2 pages)", and keep one primary email call to action repeated in header, hero and footer. No source covered PDF file naming or ATS handling of printed PDFs, nor mailto versus contact-form conversion.

## Conclusion

The central design tension is that the evidence for ATS-safe CVs (single column, plain headings, no decoration) and the trend for expressive portfolios pull in opposite directions. The page resolves it by separating layers: semantic text content stays plain and parseable, while visual flourish lives in screen-only CSS that is gated by reduced-motion and removed under `@media print`. The sourced advice on stunning design is mostly restraint. It means one visual idea, premium typography, working demos and fast loading, because reviewers punish animations that delay content and polish that outruns engineering evidence.

Several gaps limit confidence. The notes retrieved no ATS vendor documentation, no recruiter survey on summary versus skills order, no audit of award-winning portfolios, and nothing authoritative on grain, glassmorphism, cursor effects or font trends. The Firefox status of scroll-driven animations is unresolved, and the `text-wrap`, `light-dark()` and `backdrop-filter` claims are unverified. The build should therefore treat the visual details as design judgement to test in browsers and print preview. The structural choices (length, headings, case-study format, accessibility, performance and print) are the part the sources support.
